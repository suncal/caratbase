Treatment matters more than anything else

This is the single most important thing to understand about coloured stones, and the thing most owners do not know about their own. A lead-glass filled ruby and an untreated Burmese one can look alike across a counter and differ in value by a factor of thousands. Glass filling is not a minor enhancement — such a stone is substantially glass by volume, and ordinary jeweller's heat during a repair can destroy it.

Heating, by contrast, is routine, permanent and accepted. Assume any ruby or sapphire is heated unless a laboratory report says otherwise. Nearly every emerald is oiled; minor oil is normal, and only moderate or significant filling meaningfully reduces value.

Why these ranges are wider than a diamond's

Diamonds have the 4Cs and a single dominant grading authority. Coloured stones have neither. Colour quality — the thing that drives most of the value — is judged by eye, and two respected labs can describe the same stone differently. Any honest coloured stone estimate is therefore a broader range than a diamond estimate, and ours are.

When a report is worth paying for

If the calculator suggests your stone might be worth more than roughly $2,000, a laboratory report on treatment and origin will usually pay for itself several times over. Without one a buyer must assume the least favourable case and will price for that risk. How we work these figures out.
