How visits are counted

We want to know how many people use each tool. We do that without identifying anyone.

Your browser sends no identifier at all. Our server derives a one-way hash from three things — a shortened form of your IP address, your browser's user-agent string, and a secret salt that changes every day at midnight UTC — and stores only that hash.

Two consequences follow, and both are deliberate:

  • Your IP address is truncated before hashing, to the first three octets of an IPv4 address or the first four groups of an IPv6 one, so the input corresponds to a neighbourhood rather than a household. The full address is never written down.
  • Because the salt rotates daily, yesterday's hashes cannot be matched to today's. We can count how many people visited on a given day. We cannot build a profile, follow a person over time, or work backwards from stored data to an individual.

Alongside the hash we record the page path, the referring site if your browser sends one, the country your request came from as reported by our network provider, whether you are on a phone, tablet or desktop, and the time. We do not record your full IP address, your name, or anything else about you.

This is why the site has no cookie banner: there is no persistent identifier to ask your permission for.

Clearing your browser data deletes all of it, and we have no copy. This storage exists only because you asked for a feature that needs it, which is why it does not require consent.

What you actively give us

Two forms send data to our server, and only when you fill them in and submit:

  • Value-change alerts. If you give an email address we store it with the specification of the piece you valued, so we can tell you if its value moves materially. The lawful basis is your consent, which you may withdraw at any time.
  • Reported offers. If you tell us what a buyer offered you, we store the amount, the type of buyer, the stone's specification and the country. This is deliberately anonymous — it carries no email address and nothing that identifies you — and it is what allows the site to publish honest resale figures.

Where the data is held

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Analytics and form submissions are processed by a Cloudflare Worker and stored in a Cloudflare D1 database in the Asia-Pacific region. Both are processors acting on our instructions. If we later enable email delivery it will be through Resend, and this policy will be updated before that happens.

Live metal prices are fetched by our server, not by your browser, so requesting a price reveals nothing about you to those providers.

How long it is kept

  • Analytics events: 14 months, then deleted.
  • Email addresses: until you ask us to remove them, or after two years of no contact.
  • Reported offers: retained indefinitely, because they are anonymous and their value is as a long-run record. They contain nothing that identifies you.

Your rights

If you are in the UK, the EU, or a jurisdiction with comparable law such as California, you have the right to ask what we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to receive a copy, to object to processing, and to complain to your data protection authority.

In practice the only data we hold that can be linked to you is an email address you chose to give us. Write to privacy@caratbase.com and we will action it within 30 days. We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for advertising.

We cannot delete analytics records on request, because after the daily salt has rotated we have no way to work out which ones were yours. That is a consequence of the design rather than a refusal.
