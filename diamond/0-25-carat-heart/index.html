0.25 carat heart diamond: size, price and what it really resells for

A 0.25 carat heart diamond measures about 4.03 × 4.03 mm face-up and costs roughly $250–$300 at retail for a G colour, VS2, well-cut stone.

If you sold it, you would realistically be offered $75–$100 — about 33% of what it cost. A lab-grown stone of the same size and grade costs about $38.

How big it actually looks

4.03 × 4.03 mm is the face-up measurement — what sits above the finger — giving a visible area of about 16 mm². Essentially a pear with a cleft. Needs size to read clearly — rare under 0.75 ct. That is 3% less face-up area than a round of the same weight.

Carat is a measure of weight, not size, and weight rises with the cube of the length. Doubling the carat makes a stone only about 1.26 times longer, so a 0.5 carat heart would be roughly 5.1 mm across, not 8.1.

Above about G colour and VS2 clarity, almost nothing you pay for is visible without a loupe. Cut is the one grade worth protecting: a badly cut heart looks dull whatever else is true of it.

The price step just below 0.25 carat

A 0.2 carat heart of the same grade costs about $225 — 18% less than this stone — and is only 7.2% shorter across, a difference no one can see on a finger. Weights just under a round number are priced in a lower bracket because the round number itself is what buyers ask for.

The lab-grown figure is not a typo. Lab-grown prices fell by roughly 85% between 2022 and 2025 and resale is 5–12% of retail, so the saving is real at the counter and is gone the day after. Buy lab-grown to wear it; never as a store of value.
