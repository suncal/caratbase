1 carat round diamond: size, price and what it really resells for

A 1 carat round diamond measures about 6.5 × 6.5 mm face-up and costs roughly $4,575–$5,925 at retail for a G colour, VS2, well-cut stone.

If you sold it, you would realistically be offered $1,300–$2,075 — about 35% of what it cost. A lab-grown stone of the same size and grade costs about $738.

How big it actually looks

6.5 × 6.5 mm is the face-up measurement — what sits above the finger — giving a visible area of about 42 mm². The benchmark. Most weight sits in the pavilion, so it looks smaller than its carat suggests. Round is the reference shape: every other cut is measured against it.

Carat is a measure of weight, not size, and weight rises with the cube of the length. Doubling the carat makes a stone only about 1.26 times longer, so a 2 carat round would be roughly 8.2 mm across, not 13.0.

The price step just below 1 carat

A 0.95 carat round of the same grade costs about $3,838 — 27% less than this stone — and is only 1.7% shorter across, a difference no one can see on a finger. Weights just under a round number are priced in a lower bracket because the round number itself is what buyers ask for.

The lab-grown figure is not a typo. Lab-grown prices fell by roughly 85% between 2022 and 2025 and resale is 5–12% of retail, so the saving is real at the counter and is gone the day after. Buy lab-grown to wear it; never as a store of value.
