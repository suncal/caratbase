4 carat pear diamond: size, price and what it really resells for

A 4 carat pear diamond measures about 12.7 × 8.73 mm face-up and costs roughly $39,425–$51,075 at retail for a G colour, VS2, well-cut stone.

If you sold it, you would realistically be offered $11,200–$17,925 — about 35% of what it cost. A lab-grown stone of the same size and grade costs about $6,312.

How big it actually looks

12.7 × 8.73 mm is the face-up measurement — what sits above the finger — giving a visible area of about 111 mm². The longest spread of the common shapes after the marquise. That is 4% more face-up area than a round of the same weight.

Carat is a measure of weight, not size, and weight rises with the cube of the length. Doubling the carat makes a stone only about 1.26 times longer, so a 8 carat pear would be roughly 16.0 mm across, not 25.4.

Above about G colour and VS2 clarity, almost nothing you pay for is visible without a loupe. Cut is the one grade worth protecting: a badly cut pear looks dull whatever else is true of it.

The price step just below 4 carat

A 3.95 carat pear of the same grade costs about $36,700 — 19% less than this stone — and is only 0.4% shorter across, a difference no one can see on a finger. Weights just under a round number are priced in a lower bracket because the round number itself is what buyers ask for.

The lab-grown figure is not a typo. Lab-grown prices fell by roughly 85% between 2022 and 2025 and resale is 5–12% of retail, so the saving is real at the counter and is gone the day after. Buy lab-grown to wear it; never as a store of value.
