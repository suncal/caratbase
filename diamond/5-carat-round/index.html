5 carat round diamond: size, price and what it really resells for

A 5 carat round diamond measures about 11.11 × 11.11 mm face-up and costs roughly $61,600–$79,800 at retail for a G colour, VS2, well-cut stone.

If you sold it, you would realistically be offered $17,500–$28,000 — about 35% of what it cost. A lab-grown stone of the same size and grade costs about $9,862.

How big it actually looks

11.11 × 11.11 mm is the face-up measurement — what sits above the finger — giving a visible area of about 123 mm². The benchmark. Most weight sits in the pavilion, so it looks smaller than its carat suggests. Round is the reference shape: every other cut is measured against it.

Carat is a measure of weight, not size, and weight rises with the cube of the length. Doubling the carat makes a stone only about 1.26 times longer, so a 10 carat round would be roughly 14.0 mm across, not 22.2.

Above about G colour and VS2 clarity, almost nothing you pay for is visible without a loupe. Cut is the one grade worth protecting: a badly cut round looks dull whatever else is true of it.

The price step just below 5 carat

There is no useful saving just below this weight: a 4.95 carat round costs about $69,988, only 1% less, and the next price bracket does not begin until further down.

The lab-grown figure is not a typo. Lab-grown prices fell by roughly 85% between 2022 and 2025 and resale is 5–12% of retail, so the saving is real at the counter and is gone the day after. Buy lab-grown to wear it; never as a store of value.
