4 carat emerald diamond: size, price and what it really resells for

A 4 carat emerald diamond measures about 11.11 × 7.94 mm face-up and costs roughly $38,450–$49,800 at retail for a G colour, VS2, well-cut stone.

If you sold it, you would realistically be offered $10,925–$17,475 — about 35% of what it cost. A lab-grown stone of the same size and grade costs about $6,150.

How big it actually looks

11.11 × 7.94 mm is the face-up measurement — what sits above the finger — giving a visible area of about 88 mm². Step cut, so it flashes rather than sparkles. Inclusions show more readily. That is 17% less face-up area than a round of the same weight.

Carat is a measure of weight, not size, and weight rises with the cube of the length. Doubling the carat makes a stone only about 1.26 times longer, so a 8 carat emerald would be roughly 14.0 mm across, not 22.2.

Above about G colour and VS2 clarity, almost nothing you pay for is visible without a loupe. Cut is the one grade worth protecting: a badly cut emerald looks dull whatever else is true of it.

The price step just below 4 carat

A 3.95 carat emerald of the same grade costs about $35,788 — 19% less than this stone — and is only 0.4% shorter across, a difference no one can see on a finger. Weights just under a round number are priced in a lower bracket because the round number itself is what buyers ask for.

The lab-grown figure is not a typo. Lab-grown prices fell by roughly 85% between 2022 and 2025 and resale is 5–12% of retail, so the saving is real at the counter and is gone the day after. Buy lab-grown to wear it; never as a store of value.
