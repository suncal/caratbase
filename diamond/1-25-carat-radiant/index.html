1.25 carat radiant diamond: size, price and what it really resells for

A 1.25 carat radiant diamond measures about 6.68 × 5.82 mm face-up and costs roughly $4,300–$5,550 at retail for a G colour, VS2, well-cut stone.

If you sold it, you would realistically be offered $1,225–$1,950 — about 35% of what it cost. A lab-grown stone of the same size and grade costs about $688.

How big it actually looks

6.68 × 5.82 mm is the face-up measurement — what sits above the finger — giving a visible area of about 39 mm². Brilliant faceting in a rectangular outline. That is 21% less face-up area than a round of the same weight.

Carat is a measure of weight, not size, and weight rises with the cube of the length. Doubling the carat makes a stone only about 1.26 times longer, so a 2.5 carat radiant would be roughly 8.4 mm across, not 13.4.

Above about G colour and VS2 clarity, almost nothing you pay for is visible without a loupe. Cut is the one grade worth protecting: a badly cut radiant looks dull whatever else is true of it.

The price step just below 1.25 carat

There is no useful saving just below this weight: a 1.2 carat radiant costs about $4,725, only 4% less, and the next price bracket does not begin until further down.

The lab-grown figure is not a typo. Lab-grown prices fell by roughly 85% between 2022 and 2025 and resale is 5–12% of retail, so the saving is real at the counter and is gone the day after. Buy lab-grown to wear it; never as a store of value.
