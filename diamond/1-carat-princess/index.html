1 carat princess diamond: size, price and what it really resells for

A 1 carat princess diamond measures about 5.5 × 5.5 mm face-up and costs roughly $3,425–$4,450 at retail for a G colour, VS2, well-cut stone.

If you sold it, you would realistically be offered $975–$1,550 — about 35% of what it cost. A lab-grown stone of the same size and grade costs about $550.

How big it actually looks

5.5 × 5.5 mm is the face-up measurement — what sits above the finger — giving a visible area of about 30 mm². Deep, so a lot of the weight is hidden below. Small face-up for its carat. That is 28% less face-up area than a round of the same weight.

Carat is a measure of weight, not size, and weight rises with the cube of the length. Doubling the carat makes a stone only about 1.26 times longer, so a 2 carat princess would be roughly 6.9 mm across, not 11.0.

Above about G colour and VS2 clarity, almost nothing you pay for is visible without a loupe. Cut is the one grade worth protecting: a badly cut princess looks dull whatever else is true of it.

The price step just below 1 carat

A 0.95 carat princess of the same grade costs about $2,875 — 27% less than this stone — and is only 1.7% shorter across, a difference no one can see on a finger. Weights just under a round number are priced in a lower bracket because the round number itself is what buyers ask for.

The lab-grown figure is not a typo. Lab-grown prices fell by roughly 85% between 2022 and 2025 and resale is 5–12% of retail, so the saving is real at the counter and is gone the day after. Buy lab-grown to wear it; never as a store of value.
