## 1. Tool directories

**What these are actually worth.** A handful of real referring domains and some
third-party credibility. Not traffic, mostly. Reported hit rates are around nine usable
backlinks from fourteen submissions, which for a site with zero links is still the
cheapest start available. One afternoon, done once.

**Do not submit to the AI-tool directories.** TheresAnAIForThat, Futurepedia, Future Tools
and the rest of that cluster dominate the search results for "submit your tool", but we
are not an AI tool and listing there is spam. It wastes the submission and it is the kind
of thing that makes a directory distrust you.

| Where | Free? | Link | Worth it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AlternativeTo** | yes | followed | **Best of the list.** People search it for "alternative to X calculator", which is exactly our shape. List against ringsize.online, gemval, the scrap-gold calculators |
| **Product Hunt** | yes | followed | One shot, best saved for a single strong tool. The GIA reader or the offer checker |
| **Hacker News** (Show HN) | yes | followed | Free, instant, and the only one that can produce thousands of visits in a day. See below |
| **Indie Hackers** | yes | followed | Low effort, modest return, accepts post-launch updates too |
| **SaaSHub** | yes | followed | Same alternatives-discovery logic as AlternativeTo |
| **Uneed** | yes | followed | Small but genuine audience |
| **Side Projectors** | yes | followed | Low effort |
| Fazier | yes | followed *(conditional)* | Requires their badge on our site for the link to stay live. Our call whether that trade is acceptable |
| ToolPilot | yes | followed *(conditional)* | Badge required, review queue up to 90 days |
| DevHunt | yes | followed | Developer audience, weak fit for jewelry — skip unless pitching the embeddable widget |

**Order to do them in:** AlternativeTo first (highest long-term value), then SaaSHub, Uneed,
Indie Hackers, Side Projectors. Save Product Hunt and Hacker News for a deliberate launch
rather than burning them on a quiet Tuesday.

### The Show HN specifically

This is the single highest-variance item on either list. Free, takes one post, and the
realistic outcomes are *ignored* or *a few thousand visitors and the first real backlinks
this site has ever had*.

What works there is a tool that is genuinely free, needs no signup, and quietly debunks
something. Both candidates qualify:

- **The scrap gold offer checker** — "$85 per pennyweight is worth less than $60 per gram"
  is a concrete, checkable, slightly outrageous fact, and the tool proves it in one input.
- **The GIA report reader** — "a GIA report tells you what your diamond is and, by policy,
  never what it is worth" is the same shape of hook.

Pair either with the fluorescence study as the "here's the data behind it" link. Post as
`Show HN:` and be present in the comments — on HN, the thread is the product.

## 2. Guest posts and data placement

**Read this before pitching anything.** Searching "write for us jewelry" returns almost
entirely guest-post farms — sites whose business is selling placements. Their links are
cheap because they are worthless, and at volume they are a liability rather than an asset.
Everything in that category is excluded here on purpose. If a site has a "Write For Us"
page listing prices, close the tab.

### Tier 1 — trade press

Hard to land, worth disproportionately more than everything else combined. These want a
finding, not an article, and they want it from someone with a reason to know.

| Outlet | Route | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **National Jeweler** | pitch the editor | Since 1906, the industry's main US news source. **Their "Brought To You By" is paid sponsored content, not editorial — that is advertising and the link will be marked as such.** Pitch the newsroom instead |
| **JCK Online** | pitch the editor | Runs analysis and opinion from industry voices |
| **Rapaport** | pitch | The price-list people. Our fluorescence data is directly in their territory, which cuts both ways — high relevance, high scrutiny |
| **IDEX Online** | pitch | Trade-facing, data-friendly |
| **InStore Magazine** | pitch | Aimed at independent retailers, which is also our widget audience |
| **Professional Jeweler** (UK) | pitch | UK trade; the ring-size and hallmark content suits a UK readership |

**The pitch is the finding, not the site.** One paragraph, the number up front, no preamble:

> The effect of fluorescence on diamond price reverses direction depending on color
> grade, and the crossover sits between H and I. We put numbers against every grade: it
> costs about 12% at D–F and pays a small premium from I down. On a 1 ct VS1 round that
> is a $1,013 spread between a D with strong fluorescence and a D without. Happy to share
> the full tables, or write it up — it's CC licensed either way.

### Tier 2 — consumer and wedding

Larger audiences, lower authority, easier to land. The carat cliff study is the better fit
here — "a 0.99 carat E costs less than a 1.00 carat G" is a consumer-facing saving.

Wedding planning sites, engagement-ring blogs, and the personal-finance end of the market
(gold and diamonds as assets, what jewelry is really worth in an estate). Insurance and
probate blogs are an underrated adjacent vertical: they need valuation figures and have
none.

### Tier 3 — the passive route

Both studies and the price index are **CC BY 4.0 with copy-paste citations**. That is a
standing invitation: anyone can use the numbers provided they credit and link. It needs no
outreach at all and it is why the license is there.

## What this does and does not fix

Directories and a Show HN will produce a handful of links and possibly one spike. That is
worth having and it is not a traffic strategy. The thing that actually moves a site Google
has decided to ration is sustained, relevant, editorially-given links — which means the
widget outreach and the broken-link pitches in `link-building.md`, and those still need a
sending mailbox.

Nothing on this page requires one. That is the whole reason to do it first.
