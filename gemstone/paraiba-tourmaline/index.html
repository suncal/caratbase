What is a paraiba tourmaline worth?

A fine 2 carat paraiba tourmaline, heated and without certified origin, is worth roughly $43,200–$79,200 at retail — about $30,000 per carat.

Resale is far lower: $12,000–$21,000. Coloured stones have no universal grading standard, so a buyer carries more risk and prices for it.

Paraiba tourmaline value by carat

Price per carat climbs with size far faster than it does for diamonds, because large clean paraiba tourmaline is much rarer than small. A 5 carat stone of this quality is priced at about 3.2× the per-carat rate of a 1 carat one.

Treatment matters more than size

This is the single most important thing about paraiba tourmaline value, and the thing owners least often know about their own stone. For a fine 2 carat paraiba tourmaline, the same stone runs from about $33,660 (unknown) to $195,840 (untreated, lab certified) — a factor of roughly 6× — while looking much the same across a counter.

Origin, but only if certified

For paraiba tourmaline, the sources that command a premium are Brazil, Mozambique, Nigeria. A premium applies only when a recognised laboratory has certified the origin in writing. A seller's claim on its own is worth nothing.

Why the range is wide

Diamonds have the 4Cs and a dominant grading authority. Coloured stones have neither. Colour quality — which drives most of the value — is judged by eye, and two respected labs can describe the same stone differently. Any honest estimate for a paraiba tourmaline is a broader range than a diamond estimate, and ours is.

When a report pays for itself

If your stone might be worth more than about $2,000, a laboratory report on treatment and origin usually returns several times its cost. Without one a buyer must assume the least favourable case and price for that risk.
