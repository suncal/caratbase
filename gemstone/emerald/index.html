Coloured stone value

What is a emerald worth?

A fine 2 carat emerald, heated and without certified origin, is worth roughly $9,720–$17,820 at retail — about $6,750 per carat.

Resale is far lower: $2,700–$4,725. Coloured stones have no universal grading standard, so a buyer carries more risk and prices for it.

Treatment matters more than size

This is the single most important thing about emerald value, and the thing owners least often know about their own stone. An untreated stone with a laboratory report can be worth several times a heated one, and a fracture-filled or dyed stone can be worth a tiny fraction of either — while looking much the same across a counter.

Heating is routine, permanent and accepted throughout the trade. Assume any stone is heated unless a report says otherwise.

Origin, but only if certified

For emerald, the sources that command a premium are Colombia (Muzo), Zambia, Brazil. A premium applies only when a recognised laboratory has certified the origin in writing. A seller's claim on its own is worth nothing.

Why the range is wide

Diamonds have the 4Cs and a dominant grading authority. Coloured stones have neither. Colour quality — which drives most of the value — is judged by eye, and two respected labs can describe the same stone differently. Any honest estimate for a emerald is a broader range than a diamond estimate, and ours is.

When a report pays for itself

If your stone might be worth more than about $2,000, a laboratory report on treatment and origin usually returns several times its cost. Without one a buyer must assume the least favourable case and price for that risk.
